A self-hosted alternative to Unipile

EmailEngine and Unipile overlap on exactly one thing: connecting your users' mailboxes to your product. Around that overlap they are different tools: Unipile is a managed multi-channel service, EmailEngine is email-only software you run yourself. This page maps the fork honestly, because for part of what Unipile does, EmailEngine is not a candidate at all.

Built and maintained by the author of Nodemailer, the email library downloaded 16.7 million times a week. More than 200 companies run EmailEngine in production across 38 countries.

One channel or many

Unipile speaks email, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger and calendar through one API. EmailEngine does email alone, and goes deeper at it: bounce classification, mail merge, server-side templates, deliverability testing. Need the other channels? That part of the decision is already made.

Where the data lives

Unipile syncs message copies into its EU cloud (Scaleway, France), well-placed for GDPR, but still a third party holding your users' messages. EmailEngine keeps only metadata in your own Redis and fetches content on demand, so the mail never leaves your network.

How the bill scales

Unipile bills per connected account per month, and every connected service counts as an account. EmailEngine is $1,450 or €1,200 a year, flat, however many mailboxes you connect. The bills cross at about twenty accounts.

Where Unipile is the stronger choice

The first item is not a trade-off, it is a boundary: EmailEngine does not compete for it at all.

Anything beyond email

LinkedIn messaging including Recruiter and Sales Navigator, WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger. If your product needs those channels, Unipile does them and EmailEngine never will.

Nothing to operate

No server, no Redis, no monitoring, no upgrades. A managed service is the honest recommendation for a team with no ops capacity.

Universal threading

Conversations are computed in the Unipile cloud for every provider. EmailEngine has native threading only where the provider offers it: Gmail, Microsoft Graph and Yahoo.

Calendar and scheduling

Google and Outlook calendar sync with scheduling built in. EmailEngine does email, and only email.

Managed EU compliance

SOC 2, a DPA, and data kept in France. If EU-resident SaaS with vendor-held certification is what your buyers ask for, that is exactly what Unipile sells.

Official SDKs

Maintained Node.js and PHP clients. EmailEngine gives you a documented REST API and an OpenAPI spec, and the rest is on you.

Where EmailEngine is the stronger choice

If the job is email, depth beats breadth, and self-hosting answers questions a managed service cannot.

The mail never leaves your network

EU hosting still means a third party holds your users' messages. With EmailEngine, message content is fetched on demand from the original mailbox and stored on nobody's cloud, ours included.

No sub-processor to declare

Your users' mail never reaches us, so for that data we are neither controller nor processor under the GDPR. Nothing for your DPO to assess, no DPA to negotiate.

Deeper email machinery

Bounce parsing with ML-assisted classification, automatic suppression lists, mail merge, server-side templates, deliverability testing with SPF, DKIM and DMARC results. Email is the whole product, not one channel of many.

The bill ignores your growth

Per-account pricing makes your integration vendor a percentage of your revenue. EmailEngine's flat fee costs the same at fifty mailboxes and at fifty thousand.

Ships inside your product

Software rather than a service: deploy it on-premises at customer sites, air-gapped if need be, with a perpetual licence so no renewal can take a fleet down. Unipile has no self-hosted option.

Auditable source

The full source code is available for your security team to read and build. A managed API is a black box by definition.

Questions people switching actually ask

Is EmailEngine a drop-in replacement for Unipile?

For the email part, the concepts map: accounts, messages, folders, sending, webhooks, all over REST, though endpoints and payloads differ. For everything else Unipile does (LinkedIn, WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger, calendar), there is no EmailEngine equivalent to migrate to. Some teams run both: EmailEngine for deep email work, Unipile for the other channels.

Does EmailEngine support LinkedIn or WhatsApp messaging?

No. EmailEngine is email only, deliberately, and multi-channel support is not planned. If LinkedIn or WhatsApp integration is a requirement, Unipile is the right tool for that part of your product.

Unipile is EU-hosted. Isn't that enough for GDPR?

Often, yes. EU hosting with a DPA is a perfectly workable GDPR setup for many teams, and Unipile holds SOC 2 as well. The difference is structural: Unipile is still a third party holding full copies of your users' messages, so it is a sub-processor your DPO declares and assesses. EmailEngine runs on your own infrastructure and the mail never reaches us, so there is no sub-processor at all. If your policy says customer mail may not leave your network, only self-hosting satisfies it.

How do the costs actually compare?

As of mid-2026, Unipile starts at €49 a month covering up to 10 connected accounts, then roughly €5 per account per month with volume discounts, and each connected service counts separately. EmailEngine is $1,450 or €1,200 a year, flat, for unlimited mailboxes, plus the server and Redis you run it on. The bills cross at about twenty connected accounts. Both pricing pages change; check them rather than this paragraph.

What does a migration from Unipile involve?

Email accounts reconnect through EmailEngine's hosted authentication or the API, webhooks re-point to new payloads, and message data stays where it always was, in the mailboxes. Anything that is not email stays behind on Unipile. Plan a migration for the email portion, not a search-and-replace.
