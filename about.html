Built by the author of Nodemailer

EmailEngine comes from fifteen years of writing the email libraries other people build on.

Hello, I'm Andris.

I built EmailEngine, and I run Postal Systems, the company behind it.

Fifteen years of email software

I released Nodemailer in 2011 and have been working on email protocols ever since: mailparser for parsing, ImapFlow for IMAP, postal-mime for the browser, plus smtp-server and mailauth. Together they are downloaded about 788 million times a year.

Used where it is hard to get right

Those libraries are used by Microsoft, Apple, Mozilla and Atlassian, among others. EmailEngine itself runs in production at more than 200 companies across 38 countries, in CRM, SaaS and AI products.

One person, on purpose

EmailEngine is not a venture-backed team with a support tier between you and the code. When you report a bug, it reaches the person who wrote the line that caused it. That is the whole support process, and it is why a new release goes out every five days on average.

"What if you get hit by a bus?"

It is a fair question to ask a one-person vendor, and the honest answer is not "trust me". It is that EmailEngine is built so that your business does not depend on my continued existence.

It runs on your infrastructure

EmailEngine is self-hosted. There is no service of mine in the path of your email, nothing that stops working if my servers do, and no remote kill switch. An instance that is running today keeps running.

You have the source

EmailEngine is source available. Your engineers can read it, audit it and build it themselves. If you ever needed to maintain it without me, you would not be starting from a black box.

A licence that does not expire

A perpetual licence is a one-time purchase with no renewal to depend on. For teams shipping EmailEngine inside an on-premises product, that is usually the deciding factor.
